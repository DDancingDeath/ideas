# Safety, privacy and responsible learning


## 7. Safety, privacy and responsible learning

Because the primary users are children, these are core product requirements rather than features to add after launch.

## 7.1 Child safety

The tutor should:

- Stay within appropriate educational boundaries.
- Avoid sexual, violent, exploitative or otherwise age-inappropriate content.
- Avoid giving dangerous instructions during science activities.
- Never encourage secrecy from parents about safety issues.
- Avoid manipulative emotional dependency or claims that it is the child's only friend.
- Escalate concerning interactions through carefully designed safety procedures.
- Never use a child's vulnerabilities to increase engagement or spending.

The tutor should also avoid confidently inventing scientific facts or curriculum content. Ground lessons in approved sources and validate critical claims.

## 7.2 Privacy

For an India-first product, obtain legal advice on applicable requirements, including India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and associated rules as applicable to the launch date and product design.

Implement:

- Appropriate verifiable parental consent where required.
- Data minimization.
- Clear purpose limitation.
- Parent-accessible privacy settings.
- Defined retention and deletion policies.
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Access controls and audit trails.
- Vendor data-processing agreements.
- Restrictions on using children's data for model training unless specifically permitted under the applicable legal framework and consent arrangements.
- Safe handling of voice recordings, transcripts and uploaded documents.

The product should not collect family employment details, household information or other personal details just to make examples more relatable. Allow parents and children to choose familiar contexts themselves.

## 7.3 Protect the child from being over-assessed

Continuous assessment does not mean testing every interaction.

Use a mixture of informal observation, interactive activities and targeted checks
without turning the experience into an examination.

The system should be able to teach without turning every conversation into a score.

## 7.4 Prevent false aptitude judgments

Interest discovery is useful only if presented honestly.

The system should report:

- What it observed.
- How often it observed it.
- In which contexts.
- How confident the inference is.
- What further activity could test the hypothesis.

It should not present an unvalidated prediction as a scientific measurement of the child's future ability.
