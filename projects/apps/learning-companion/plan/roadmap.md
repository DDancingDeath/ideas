# Phased implementation roadmap

Phase labels in the supplied roadmap are milestone identifiers M1–M5. Durations are indicative estimates, not commitments. The first demonstrable experience is a scope proposal, not a request to build it in this repository.

## 9. Implementation roadmap

Do not attempt to build the entire vision at once. Your first release should prove that the learning loop works before you invest in elaborate 3D experiences, career guidance or broad curriculum coverage.

## Phase 1 — Working prototype

First milestone

Indicative duration: 3–5 weeks for a focused prototype with AI-assisted development, depending on experience and available time.

Build one complete learning journey for one grade and subject.

### Deliverables

- Parent creates a learner profile.
- A curated curriculum is available.
- The student completes a short diagnostic.
- The application generates a basic study plan.
- The student completes an interactive lesson.
- The AI explains concepts and asks follow-up questions.
- The student receives immediate feedback.
- The application stores progress.
- The parent can see what was learned and what needs attention.

### Exit criteria

A child can complete a lesson, close the application, return later and continue without losing progress. The system can distinguish between a lesson viewed and a concept demonstrated.

Do not add career profiling or elaborate gamification yet.

## Phase 2 — Adaptive learning MVP

Indicative duration: an additional 4–8 weeks.

Add:

- Concept-level mastery tracking.
- Multiple teaching strategies.
- Prerequisite-aware progression.
- Hints and alternative explanations.
- Question banks with validated answers.
- Spaced revision.
- Mixed-topic quizzes.
- Parent dashboard.
- Basic deadline-aware scheduling.
- Automatic replanning after missed sessions.

At this stage, the product should demonstrate that it can teach the same concept differently to different learners and record whether the change helped.

## Phase 3 — Visual and engaging learning

Indicative duration: an additional 4–8 weeks, depending on the media scope.

Add:

- Interactive diagrams and reusable visualizations.
- Story-based lessons.
- Puzzle-based learning.
- Narration and speech input.
- A progression from exploration to formal content.
- Optional advanced challenges.
- A library of approved animations and videos.

Prioritize reusable visual components over bespoke media production.

## Phase 4 — Full curriculum management

Indicative duration: several additional months, with scope depending heavily on curriculum breadth.

Add:

- Automated document ingestion.
- Curriculum version management.
- More chapters and subjects.
- Deeper prerequisite mapping.
- Deadline feasibility analysis.
- Exam simulation.
- Comprehensive cumulative revision.
- Stronger content review and analytics.

This is where content quality and curriculum validation become a major ongoing workload, not just a software problem.

## Phase 5 — Long-term learner development

Add:

- Evidence-based interest profiles.
- Optional enrichment pathways.
- Cross-subject connections.
- Recommendations for experiments and projects.
- Parent-facing developmental insights.
- Longitudinal progress across academic years.
- Carefully validated guidance for future subject exploration.

This phase should come after you have enough learning history to make useful inferences.

### Overall build estimate

For a solo developer using AI coding tools, a credible prototype may be achievable in several weeks. A dependable adaptive-learning MVP is more realistically a multi-month project. A polished, broad, child-safe product with high-quality curriculum content, multilingual support and meaningful long-term analytics could take a year or more with a small team.

These are planning estimates, not commitments. Existing engineering experience, use of managed services, available curriculum content and the quality bar will materially affect the schedule.

## 14. What I would build first

If I were leading this project, I would start with one complete, measurable experience.

## The first demonstrable experience

1. A parent creates a learner profile and chooses a grade and target date.
2. The app starts with a short mathematics diagnostic.
3. It generates a plan for a small, curated set of concepts.
4. The child learns fractions through a story and interactive visuals.
5. The tutor asks questions during the lesson and adapts when the child struggles.
6. The child solves independent problems and returns for a later revision.
7. The parent sees what the child understood, what remains uncertain and what the tutor plans to do next.

This one experience tests almost every foundational hypothesis in your idea: curriculum structure, scheduling, AI tutoring, adaptive teaching, assessment, revision, visuals and parent visibility.

If it works, expand to additional concepts and subjects. If it doesn't, you can discover why without having invested in a massive platform.
